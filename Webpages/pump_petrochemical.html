Investigation of Increasing Capacities of Centrifugal Pumps By CFD Analysis

Comprehensive CFD Study of Impeller Redesign for P-202, P-204, P-401, and P-604 Pumps

Project Overview

This comprehensive research project investigates the capacity enhancement of four critical centrifugal pumps (P-202, P-204, P-401, and P-604) through advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analysis. The primary objective is to evaluate the performance improvement achieved by replacing original impellers with re-impelled designs featuring modified geometric parameters, including increased impeller diameter and optimized blade configurations. The study employs rigorous numerical simulation using ANSYS CFX to predict pump performance, validate results against operational data, and generate complete performance curves for the redesigned pumps.

Research Objectives

  • Evaluate capacity increase potential through impeller diameter modification
  • Validate CFD simulation methodology against online operational data
  • Generate comprehensive performance curves for original and re-impelled pumps
  • Assess head delivery capability for increased process capacity requirements
  • Determine hydraulic efficiency and power consumption characteristics
  • Compare simulated performance with manufacturer specifications

CFD Simulation Methodology

Computational Approach

The computational fluid dynamics analysis employed ANSYS CFX commercial software to simulate both original and re-impelled pump configurations. The simulation methodology involved comprehensive three-dimensional flow field modeling within the pump impeller and volute casing, incorporating turbulence modeling, rotating reference frame treatment for impeller motion, and steady-state flow analysis. Mesh generation utilized structured and unstructured grid techniques optimized for rotating machinery applications, with particular attention to boundary layer resolution in critical flow regions including blade surfaces and volute tongue areas.

Validation Procedure

Model validation constituted a critical component of the research methodology. For each original pump configuration (P-202, P-204, P-401, P-604), CFD simulation results were rigorously compared against online operational data obtained from the plant distributed control system (DCS). Validation parameters included flow rate, developed head, shaft power consumption, electrical current, voltage, and overall pump efficiency. Acceptable agreement between simulated and measured performance established the credibility of the numerical model for subsequent analysis of re-impelled configurations.

Performance Curve Generation

Complete performance curves (head-capacity, power-capacity, and efficiency-capacity) were generated for all re-impelled pump configurations through systematic CFD analysis across multiple operating points. Simulations spanned the complete operating range from minimum to maximum flow rates, establishing characteristic curves that could be directly compared with manufacturer data. Operating point analysis positioned original pump duty points on both manufacturer curves and simulated curves, enabling comprehensive performance evaluation and capacity increase assessment.

Critical Finding: Complete Head Delivery Capability

The comprehensive CFD analysis confirms that all re-impelled pump configurations (P-202, P-204, P-401, and P-604) possess the hydraulic capability to deliver the required head at increased process capacity flow rates. This validates the technical feasibility of the impeller redesign approach for achieving the targeted capacity enhancement objectives without requiring complete pump replacement or major modifications to existing pump installations.

Detailed Performance Analysis: P-202 Re-Impelled Pump

For the P-202 re-impelled configuration, detailed CFD analysis across the complete operating range reveals optimal performance characteristics. Maximum shaft power occurs at 140 m³/h flow rate, representing the upper limit of the operating envelope. Hydraulic efficiency varies from 67% at minimum flow (20 m³/h) to a peak efficiency of 86% at 75 m³/h, demonstrating excellent efficiency characteristics across a broad operating range. This efficiency profile indicates that the re-impelled design maintains high hydraulic performance while delivering substantially increased flow capacity compared to the original configuration.

Technical Achievements and Industrial Implications

Key Research Contributions

  • Validated CFD Methodology: Established accurate numerical simulation approach validated against operational data for centrifugal pump performance prediction
  • Capacity Enhancement Confirmation: Demonstrated 10.6% to 59.6% flow rate increase across four critical pumps through impeller redesign
  • Head Delivery Validation: Confirmed all re-impelled configurations deliver required head at increased flow rates
  • Performance Curve Generation: Developed complete characteristic curves spanning full operating range for engineering design
  • Efficiency Characterization: Quantified hydraulic efficiency profiles across operating range for optimized pump selection
  • Power Consumption Analysis: Determined shaft power requirements for electrical infrastructure planning
  • Manufacturer Comparison: Validated simulated performance against manufacturer data for design confidence
  • Cost-Effective Solution: Enabled capacity increase through impeller replacement rather than complete pump replacement

Industrial Implementation Considerations

The research provides a complete engineering foundation for implementing capacity enhancement through re-impelled pump installations. The validated CFD methodology enables confident prediction of pump performance prior to physical implementation, reducing technical risk and enabling informed investment decisions. The confirmed head delivery capability at increased flow rates validates that existing pump casings, driver systems, and piping infrastructure can accommodate the enhanced capacity with impeller replacement as the primary modification. Power consumption analysis informs electrical system adequacy assessment and potential motor upgrade requirements.

Economic and Operational Benefits

Impeller replacement represents a significantly more economical approach to capacity enhancement compared to complete pump replacement, which would require extensive piping modifications, foundation work, and extended plant shutdown periods. The ability to achieve 10.6% to 59.6% capacity increases through targeted impeller redesign enables process debottlenecking and production increase with minimal capital investment and installation downtime. The comprehensive performance characterization ensures the re-impelled pumps operate within acceptable efficiency ranges, maintaining acceptable operating costs despite increased throughput.

Conclusions

This comprehensive CFD investigation successfully demonstrates the technical and economic feasibility of centrifugal pump capacity enhancement through strategic impeller redesign. The rigorous numerical simulation methodology, validated against operational data, provides high confidence in predicted performance for re-impelled configurations. The research confirms that geometric modifications, particularly increased impeller diameter and optimized blade geometry, deliver substantial flow rate increases (10.6% to 59.6%) while maintaining adequate head delivery across all four investigated pumps (P-202, P-204, P-401, P-604).

Critical findings establish that all re-impelled pump configurations possess the hydraulic capability to deliver required heads at increased process capacity flow rates, validating the approach for industrial implementation. Detailed performance characterization, including complete characteristic curves and efficiency profiles, provides the engineering foundation necessary for confident design decisions and operational planning. The methodology developed in this research demonstrates the powerful application of computational fluid dynamics for rotating machinery performance prediction and optimization in industrial applications.

Research Impact

The integration of advanced CFD simulation with operational validation establishes a proven methodology for pump performance enhancement assessment applicable across diverse industrial facilities. The demonstrated capacity increases through impeller modification provide a cost-effective debottlenecking solution that avoids the capital expenditure and installation complexity of complete pump replacement projects. The research delivers actionable engineering data supporting informed investment decisions for process capacity enhancement initiatives.
