Rotary Piston Pump Design and Numerical Simulation

CFD Analysis and Performance Optimization for High-Pressure Seawater Pumping Applications

Project Overview

This project encompasses the design, numerical simulation, and performance optimization of a specialized rotary piston pump for seawater pumping applications up to 35 bar pressure. The pump design employs reverse engineering methodology, where the fluid chamber volume and rotational speed are calculated based on required flow rate and estimated volumetric efficiency.

Given the unique nature of this pump design and the absence of prior numerical or analytical studies in the literature, a systematic validation approach was implemented using lobe pump simulations as benchmark cases before analyzing the main pump configuration.

Design Methodology

The pump design utilizes a reverse engineering approach based on performance specifications. The methodology involves calculating the pump chamber volume and rotational speed from target flow rate and volumetric efficiency requirements.

Performance Parameters Analysis

Two critical parameters significantly influence pump performance:

  • Rotational Speed: Flow rate increases significantly with pump rotational speed, providing direct control over pumping capacity
  • Rotor-Casing Clearance: Increased clearance leads to higher internal leakage between drive rotor sides, resulting in reduced flow rate and volumetric efficiency

Critical Design Consideration

Clearance management is essential for maintaining high volumetric efficiency. Internal leakage through excessive clearance can significantly compromise pump performance, making precision manufacturing crucial for optimal operation.

Numerical Simulation Strategy

Given the unique nature of this pump design and the absence of prior numerical or analytical studies in the literature, a systematic two-phase validation approach was implemented using ANSYS Fluent.

Phase 1: Validation Studies

Initial simulations focused on established lobe pump configurations to develop and validate the numerical framework:

  • Lobe pump numerical simulation as benchmark case
  • Investigation of appropriate boundary conditions and mesh configurations
  • Mesh refinement and remeshing strategy optimization
  • Time-dependent flow rate curve generation and validation against published literature
  • Analysis of cosine deviation effects in lobe geometry

Validation Results

The lobe pump simulations successfully reproduced flow characteristics reported in literature, demonstrating the reliability of the numerical setup. Flow rate variations at different rotor angles (45°, 90°, 135° at 600 rpm) matched expected sinusoidal, circular, and involute patterns.

Phase 2: Main Pump Analysis

Following successful validation, the proven numerical methodology was applied to the specialized rotary piston pump:

  • Application of validated numerical setup to the primary pump design
  • Comprehensive CFD analysis of internal flow patterns and pressure distributions
  • Performance prediction across operating range
  • Optimization of geometric parameters for viscous fluid handling
  • Assessment of suitability for offshore and firefighting applications

Key CFD Findings

Velocity vector analysis revealed complex flow patterns within the pump chambers, including vortex formation and secondary flows. The simulations provided detailed insights into pressure development through the pumping cycle and identified optimal operating conditions for maximum efficiency.

Key Findings and Results

The comprehensive numerical study systematically examined the effects of various design and operating parameters:

Rotational Speed Effects

Flow rate characteristics were analyzed at multiple rotor positions (45°, 90°, 135°) at a constant speed of 600 rpm. The results demonstrated predictable flow rate variations corresponding to the pump's geometric configuration and rotor positioning.

Geometric Configuration Analysis

Three distinct lobe geometries were evaluated:

  • Sinusoidal Profile: Smooth flow characteristics with minimal pulsation
  • Circular Profile: Balanced performance between flow smoothness and volumetric efficiency
  • Involute Profile: Enhanced sealing characteristics reducing internal leakage

Flow Pattern Visualization

Velocity vector analysis provided critical insights into internal fluid dynamics, revealing secondary flow structures and their impact on overall pump efficiency. These visualizations enabled optimization of chamber geometry for improved performance.

Project Outcome

This research provides essential design guidelines and validated numerical modeling approaches for specialized rotary pumps in high-pressure seawater applications. The CFD methodologies developed are applicable to similar positive displacement pump designs, enabling efficient development of custom pumping solutions for demanding industrial applications including viscous fluid handling, firefighting systems, and offshore operations.
