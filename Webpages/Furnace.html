Advanced Coke Recycling System for Cracking Furnaces

CFD Simulation, Thermal-Structural Analysis, and Energy-Exergy Optimization for Olefin Production

Project Overview

This comprehensive research project investigates the feasibility and optimization of coke combustion systems for ethylene production cracking furnaces through advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation, coupled thermal-structural analysis, and rigorous energy-exergy evaluation. The study addresses the critical challenge of coke formation during ethane-to-ethylene cracking operations and develops an innovative closed-loop injection system that transforms waste coke into a valuable supplementary fuel source. The project encompasses complete thermal, hydrodynamic, and structural behavior modeling of the furnace combustion chamber, coke trap system, and injection nozzle assembly under both cracking and De-Coke operational modes.

Challenges with Conventional De-Coke Operations

In steam cracking furnaces used for olefin production, coke deposition on the inner walls of radiant coils is an inevitable consequence of the thermal cracking process. After 30-45 days of continuous operation, approximately 1,140 kg of coke accumulates during a typical 44-hour decoking cycle. Conventional decoking methodology employs steam and air injection to extract coke particles, which are then separated via cyclone filtration systems. This traditional approach presents multiple technical, economic, and environmental challenges that significantly impact operational efficiency and sustainability.

Critical Operational Challenges

  • Production Interruption: Extended 48-hour shutdowns per decoking cycle result in substantial capacity loss and reduced annual throughput
  • Economic Burden: Comprehensive costs encompassing extraction, cyclone filtration, transportation, storage, and final disposal (incineration or landfill)
  • Environmental Impact: Offsite disposal generates significant CO₂ emissions and environmental contamination, creating regulatory compliance challenges
  • Equipment Deterioration: Abrasive coke particles cause accelerated wear on downstream equipment, heat exchangers, and transfer lines, escalating maintenance requirements
  • Infrastructure Requirements: Extensive site footprint for collection systems, temporary storage facilities, and material handling equipment
  • Safety Concerns: Handling and transportation of particulate waste presents occupational health and safety risks

Advanced Closed-Loop Combustion Technology

The innovative system fundamentally reimagines coke management by implementing a thermally integrated closed-loop approach. The technology leverages the inherent calorific value of coke (98.4% fixed carbon content) as a supplementary fuel source through a sophisticated coke trap and injection system. During decoking operations, coke particles are collected and subsequently reinjected into the combustion chamber through precisely engineered injection nozzles positioned at the furnace floor. This approach achieves complete (100%) coke conversion when injected with controlled airflow, as validated through comprehensive CFD modeling.

Coke Trap and Injection System Design

The coke trap system features a three-dimensional geometry optimized for particle flow dynamics and thermal distribution. Laboratory sampling and characterization of actual coke particles from industrial operations established the particle size distribution boundary conditions applied at the coke trap inlet. CFD simulation of the coke trap hydrodynamic and thermal behavior determined the optimal outlet conditions for injection nozzles. The system architecture features strategically positioned injection nozzles (8-inch diameter) distributed across the furnace floor between the radiant coils and furnace walls, with each furnace zone containing 2 injection points. Particle velocity distribution analysis within the coke trap and injection nozzles confirms uniform flow characteristics and stable injection performance.

CFD Modeling and Operational Performance

Comprehensive computational modeling employed Realizable k-ε turbulence models, Discrete Ordinates radiation modeling, and intrinsic kinetic models for coke combustion kinetics using ANSYS Fluent. Comparative analysis evaluated furnace performance under three operational scenarios: conventional cracking mode, De-Coke process without coke injection, and De-Coke process with coke injection. Results demonstrated that injected coke particles achieve complete combustion, with maximum furnace temperatures occurring during coke injection operations. Temperature and velocity distributions throughout the furnace reveal optimal thermal uniformity and gas flow patterns that support complete particle conversion.

Thermal-Structural Analysis and Material Selection

Critical to system viability is the structural integrity of the injection nozzle connection to the furnace floor under extreme thermal and mechanical loading. Finite element analysis (FEA) evaluated heat transfer, temperature distribution, and thermal stress in the connection assembly components. Initial design concepts involving direct welding of the injection tube to the furnace floor plate generated excessive thermal stress concentrations that would cause premature failure. The optimized design implements an intermediate sleeve (insulating sleeve) connected to the furnace floor through a bolted flange assembly, eliminating direct tube-to-floor welding. This configuration maintains thermal stresses within acceptable material limits while providing structural stability under cyclic thermal loading during cracking and De-Coke operations.

Stress Analysis and Connection Optimization

Detailed stress-strain analysis compared multiple connection configurations including simple annular plate, annular plate with 8 slots, and annular plate with 16 slots. Von Mises stress distributions and X-component strain evaluations determined optimal geometry for stress distribution and thermal expansion accommodation. The final design specification utilizes a bolted flange connection between the insulating sleeve and furnace floor, preventing direct contact between the injection tube and floor plate. This eliminates the weld-induced stress concentration while maintaining secure mechanical attachment and thermal isolation. Strain analysis confirms that the non-welded configuration with intermediate sleeve maintains strain levels well below material failure thresholds across all operational conditions.

Quantified Technical Achievements

  • Complete Combustion: 100% coke particle conversion achieved through optimized injection parameters and residence time
  • Thermal Management: Maximum furnace temperature reached during coke injection, providing enhanced heat transfer to radiant coils
  • Structural Integrity: Bolted flange connection design eliminates weld-induced stress failures and accommodates thermal expansion
  • Material Optimization: Insulating sleeve design maintains thermal stresses within acceptable limits for selected materials
  • Energy Recovery: Utilizes coke's calorific value as supplementary fuel, reducing primary fuel consumption
  • Zero Waste Generation: Eliminates 1,140 kg of coke waste per decoking cycle
  • Environmental Compliance: Avoids emissions from external waste incineration while recovering energy within the integrated process
  • Operational Flexibility: System accommodates both cracking and De-Coke operations without structural modifications

Energy-Exergy Analysis and Thermodynamic Performance

Comprehensive thermodynamic evaluation compared three operational scenarios: conventional cracking, standard De-Coke process, and burning-coke De-Coke process. Energy analysis reveals that the combustion furnace achieves 44.8% energy efficiency during cracking operations, with 45% of input energy transferred to the radiant coils[1]. During burning-coke De-Coke operations, although energy transfer to coils decreases to approximately 8%, the process recovers significant thermal energy that would otherwise require external disposal infrastructure.

Exergy analysis provides critical insights into thermodynamic quality and irreversibility. The combustion furnace demonstrates 29% exergy efficiency during cracking with approximately 36% exergy destruction due to combustion irreversibilities[1]. In contrast, the radiant coils exhibit superior performance with 96.2% exergy efficiency and less than 4% exergy destruction during cracking operations. The burning-coke De-Coke process experiences elevated exergy destruction (approximately 60%) attributed to secondary combustion processes, yet eliminates the exergy losses associated with external waste treatment systems.

Comparative Analysis: Temperature Sensitivity vs. Batch Injection

Two operational strategies were systematically evaluated[1]: (1) increasing De-Coke stream temperature from 454 K to 654 K while maintaining continuous injection, and (2) batch injection with controlled air flow rates (10-30% of nominal De-Coke flow). Strategy 1 achieved only marginal improvement in coke conversion (52% to 55%) due to competing effects of increased temperature and reduced residence time from elevated gas velocities. Strategy 2 demonstrated superior performance with 100% coke conversion across all tested air flow rates, attributed to significantly elevated furnace temperatures (1036-1277 K) and optimized residence times (3.16-3.89 seconds).

Key Research Findings and Industrial Implications

The integrated CFD and FEA modeling validates complete technical feasibility of direct coke combustion in cracking furnaces. The comprehensive analysis spanning particle flow dynamics in the coke trap system, combustion performance in the furnace chamber, and thermal-structural integrity of injection connections provides a complete engineering solution. Critical design insight revealed that welded connections between injection tubes and furnace floor generate unacceptable stress concentrations leading to premature failure. The optimized bolted flange connection with intermediate insulating sleeve successfully manages thermal stresses while maintaining structural integrity across all operational modes. The technology delivers simultaneous benefits: elimination of 100% waste disposal requirements, recovery of coke's inherent calorific value, significant reduction in environmental emissions, removal of costly waste management infrastructure, and enhanced operational flexibility for ethylene production facilities.

Environmental and Economic Impact

The closed-loop coke recycling system represents a paradigm shift toward circular economy principles in petrochemical operations[2]. By transforming a regulated waste stream into an in-process fuel source, the technology simultaneously addresses environmental compliance, operational efficiency, and economic performance objectives. The elimination of 1,140 kg of coke waste per cycle, prevention of particulate emissions during handling and transport, and avoidance of external incineration-related CO₂ emissions contribute to substantial carbon footprint reduction and enhanced sustainability metrics for olefin production facilities. The optimized mechanical design ensures long-term reliability and eliminates recurring maintenance issues associated with thermal stress-induced failures in welded connections.
