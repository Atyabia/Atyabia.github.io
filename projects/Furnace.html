Innovative Coke Recycling System

Closed-Loop Solution for Ethylene-Ethane Cracking Furnaces

Project Overview

This research project presents a comprehensive feasibility study of an innovative coke combustion and recycling system for ethylene production cracking furnaces in olefin units. Utilizing advanced computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulation coupled with rigorous energy-exergy analysis, this study investigates the technical and thermodynamic viability of burning coke particles directly within the furnace during the De-Coke process. The patented closed-loop system employs a specialized "coke trap" mechanism to reinject extracted coke particles back into the combustion chamber, transforming a waste management challenge into an efficient fuel recovery process.

Challenges with Conventional De-Coke Operations

In steam cracking furnaces used for olefin production, coke deposition on the inner walls of radiant coils is an inevitable consequence of the thermal cracking process. After 30-45 days of continuous operation, approximately 1,140 kg of coke accumulates during a typical 44-hour decoking cycle. Conventional decoking methodology employs steam and air injection to extract coke particles, which are then separated via cyclone filtration systems. This traditional approach presents multiple technical, economic, and environmental challenges that significantly impact operational efficiency and sustainability.

Critical Operational Challenges

  • Production Interruption: Extended 48-hour shutdowns per decoking cycle result in substantial capacity loss and reduced annual throughput
  • Economic Burden: Comprehensive costs encompassing extraction, cyclone filtration, transportation, storage, and final disposal (incineration or landfill)
  • Environmental Impact: Offsite disposal generates significant CO₂ emissions and environmental contamination, creating regulatory compliance challenges
  • Equipment Deterioration: Abrasive coke particles cause accelerated wear on downstream equipment, heat exchangers, and transfer lines, escalating maintenance requirements
  • Infrastructure Requirements: Extensive site footprint for collection systems, temporary storage facilities, and material handling equipment
  • Safety Concerns: Handling and transportation of particulate waste presents occupational health and safety risks

Advanced Closed-Loop Combustion Technology

The patented innovation fundamentally reimagines coke management by implementing a thermally integrated closed-loop system. Rather than treating extracted coke as waste requiring disposal, the technology leverages the inherent calorific value of coke (98.4% fixed carbon content) as a supplementary fuel source. During decoking operations, coke particles are collected in an engineered storage vessel and subsequently reinjected into the combustion chamber through a proprietary coke trap injection system. This approach achieves complete (100%) coke conversion when injected with controlled airflow, as validated through comprehensive CFD modeling using ANSYS Fluent with Euler-Lagrange multiphase flow simulation.

Technical Design Specifications

The system architecture features strategically positioned injection nozzles (8-inch diameter) distributed across 12 injection points on the furnace floor between the radiant coils and furnace walls. The coke trap connection utilizes a high-temperature resistant junction designed to withstand furnace operating conditions (temperatures up to 1277 K) without structural compromise or thermal stress failure. The injection system employs controlled pneumatic conveyance with optimized air flow rates (10-30% of nominal De-Coke flow) to achieve complete particle combustion while maintaining furnace temperature uniformity and minimizing velocity-induced residence time reduction.

CFD Simulation and Performance Validation

Computational modeling employed Realizable k-ε turbulence models, Discrete Ordinates radiation modeling, and intrinsic kinetic models for coke combustion kinetics. Simulation results demonstrated that particle diameter reduction from 50 μm to complete burnout occurs within 6-11 meters of furnace height depending on injection parameters. The optimal configuration (10% air injection flow rate) achieved furnace temperatures of 1277 K with particle residence times of 3.16 seconds, ensuring complete combustion and CO₂ generation rates of 0.018 mole fraction.

Quantified Performance Benefits

  • Energy Recovery: Utilizes coke's calorific value (98.4% fixed carbon, 2490.92 m²/kg specific surface area) as supplementary fuel, reducing primary fuel consumption
  • Zero Waste Generation: Eliminates 1,140 kg of coke waste per decoking cycle, removing all offsite transportation and disposal requirements
  • Carbon Footprint Reduction: Avoids emissions from waste incineration facilities while recovering energy within the integrated process
  • Space Optimization: Closed-loop configuration eliminates cyclone separators, storage silos, and material handling infrastructure
  • Equipment Preservation: Prevents abrasive particle damage to downstream process equipment and heat transfer surfaces
  • Operational Continuity: Batch injection process allows flexible coke combustion scheduling without extended production interruptions
  • Thermodynamic Efficiency: Energy efficiency of 44.8% and exergy efficiency of 29% for combustion furnace during burning-coke De-Coke process

Energy-Exergy Analysis and Thermodynamic Performance

Comprehensive thermodynamic evaluation compared three operational scenarios: conventional cracking, standard De-Coke process, and burning-coke De-Coke process. Energy analysis reveals that the combustion furnace achieves 44.8% energy efficiency during cracking operations, with 45% of input energy transferred to the radiant coils[1]. During burning-coke De-Coke operations, although energy transfer to coils decreases to approximately 8%, the process recovers significant thermal energy that would otherwise require external disposal infrastructure.

Exergy analysis provides critical insights into thermodynamic quality and irreversibility. The combustion furnace demonstrates 29% exergy efficiency during cracking with approximately 36% exergy destruction due to combustion irreversibilities[1]. In contrast, the radiant coils exhibit superior performance with 96.2% exergy efficiency and less than 4% exergy destruction during cracking operations. The burning-coke De-Coke process experiences elevated exergy destruction (approximately 60%) attributed to secondary combustion processes, yet eliminates the exergy losses associated with external waste treatment systems.

Comparative Analysis: Temperature Sensitivity vs. Batch Injection

Two operational strategies were systematically evaluated[1]: (1) increasing De-Coke stream temperature from 454 K to 654 K while maintaining continuous injection, and (2) batch injection with controlled air flow rates (10-30% of nominal De-Coke flow). Strategy 1 achieved only marginal improvement in coke conversion (52% to 55%) due to competing effects of increased temperature and reduced residence time from elevated gas velocities. Strategy 2 demonstrated superior performance with 100% coke conversion across all tested air flow rates, attributed to significantly elevated furnace temperatures (1036-1277 K) and optimized residence times (3.16-3.89 seconds).

Key Research Findings and Industrial Implications

The validated CFD model confirms that direct coke combustion in the furnace achieves complete particle conversion while maintaining thermal operation within acceptable parameters. Although burning-coke De-Coke processes exhibit reduced energy and exergy transfer efficiency to radiant coils compared to conventional methods, the technology delivers substantial benefits: elimination of 100% waste disposal requirements, recovery of coke's inherent calorific value, significant reduction in environmental emissions from external incineration, and elimination of costly waste management infrastructure. The optimal operational parameters identified (10% air injection at 654 K De-Coke stream temperature) provide practical guidance for industrial implementation in ethylene production facilities worldwide.

Environmental and Economic Impact

The closed-loop coke recycling system represents a paradigm shift toward circular economy principles in petrochemical operations[2]. By transforming a regulated waste stream into an in-process fuel source, the technology simultaneously addresses environmental compliance, operational efficiency, and economic performance objectives. The elimination of 1,140 kg of coke waste per cycle, prevention of particulate emissions during handling and transport, and avoidance of external incineration-related CO₂ emissions contribute to substantial carbon footprint reduction and enhanced sustainability metrics for olefin production facilities.
